\abstract{Claims that one machine learning model outperforms another usually rest on cross-validated scores and a test that assumes independent observations. On spatial and temporal data this assumption fails twice: random cross-validation leaks information between neighbouring observations, and the prediction errors of the compared models are themselves dependent. We study the second, less examined problem. We cast model comparison as inference on the mean of a dependent field of out-of-fold loss differentials with correlated fold-level training effects, and show that the paired $t$-test on fold scores underestimates the variance by exactly the average covariance between fold means. We propose DA-HAC, a dependence-aware test computed from a single blocked cross-validation run. It combines a product-kernel variance estimator with bandwidths calibrated on the empirical variogram of the loss differentials, a method-of-moments estimate of the training variance, and a Satterthwaite reference distribution. In simulations with exact control of the truth, including XGBoost learners whose expected performances are equal by construction, conventional procedures reject a true null hypothesis in up to $80\%$ of replicates, and random cross-validation halves the estimated gain of a genuinely better model under strong dependence. Blocked cross-validation with DA-HAC keeps the error rate near the nominal level under weak dependence and reduces it substantially otherwise, while diagnosing, through an effective sample size and a range ratio, the cases in which no reliable comparison is possible. On hourly weather, pedestrian counts and yield-monitor data, the conclusions of four of six comparisons depend on these choices. An open-source Python implementation is provided.}
