\section{Simulation study II: learners on spatiotemporal data}\label{sec:sim2}

The second study reproduces the full pipeline of an applied comparison: data generation, model fitting by cross-validation and testing. The difficulty in such a study is to know the truth. We obtain an exact null by exchangeability.

\subsection{Design}

\paragraph{Data.} On a $10\times10$ grid observed at $20$ time points ($n=2000$), three covariates $x_1,x_2,x_3$ and a latent residual $w$ are independent spatiotemporal fields with the dependence levels of Section~\ref{sec:sim1}. The response is
\[
y=g(x_1)+(1-\gamma)\,g(x_2)+x_3+w+\epsilon,\qquad g(v)=\sin(2v)+v,\qquad \epsilon\sim N(0,0.5^2)\ \text{i.i.d.}
\]
\paragraph{Models.} Model $A$ is XGBoost~\cite{chen2016} ($100$ trees of depth $4$, learning rate $0.1$, row subsampling $0.8$) fitted on $(x_1,x_3,s,t)$; model $B$ is the same learner fitted on $(x_2,x_3,s,t)$. Including the coordinates lets both models exploit the spatial and temporal structure of $w$, as is common in practice.

\paragraph{Exact null by exchangeability.} When $\gamma=0$, the joint distribution of the data is invariant under the exchange of $x_1$ and $x_2$, which exchanges the roles of $A$ and $B$. Hence $\E[d_i]=0$ for every observation and every validation design: the two models have exactly the same expected performance, although in any given sample one of them will appear better. When $\gamma>0$, $x_1$ carries more signal than $x_2$ and $A$ is truly better; we use $\gamma\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$.

\paragraph{Validation and procedures.} Two pipelines are applied to each data set. The \emph{conventional} pipeline uses random $5$-fold cross-validation with the paired $t$-test on fold means and the i.i.d. bootstrap, and $5\times5$ repeated random cross-validation with the corrected resampled $t$-test~\cite{nadeau2003}. The \emph{dependence-aware} pipeline uses blocked $5$-fold cross-validation ($5\times5$-site tiles crossed with $4$-step windows, $G=20$ blocks, and a buffer that removes from the training set observations within one site and one time step of the test fold) with the procedures of Section~\ref{sec:sim1}, including DA-P with $\rho_0=0$ and $\rho_0=0.5$. There are $300$ replicates per dependence level under the null and $150$ under each alternative.

\subsection{Results}

\paragraph{Random cross-validation.} The left panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:exp2size} and the upper part of Table~\ref{tab:exp2} show that the conventional pipeline fails as soon as the data are dependent. With random $5$-fold cross-validation, the paired $t$-test rejects a true null in $15\%$ of replicates under moderate and $29\%$ under strong dependence, and the i.i.d. bootstrap in $24\%$ and $40\%$. The corrected resampled $t$-test of Nadeau and Bengio is well calibrated without dependence ($3.7\%$) and under weak dependence ($5.7\%$), as designed, but not beyond ($16\%$ and $33\%$): its correction accounts for overlapping training sets, not for dependent test observations. Note also that even without spatiotemporal dependence the paired $t$-test rejects $9\%$ of true nulls, the problem identified by Dietterich~\cite{dietterich1998}.

\paragraph{The estimand changes with the validation design.} Random cross-validation does not only produce invalid tests; it also estimates a different quantity. Table~\ref{tab:exp2est} reports the average estimated gain of model $A$ under both designs. Because both models can exploit the coordinates to interpolate between nearby training observations, random cross-validation attenuates the advantage conferred by the more informative covariate, by about $30\%$ under moderate and $50\%$ under strong dependence ($-0.53$ instead of $-0.80$, and $-0.30$ instead of $-0.60$, for $\gamma=0.2$). A gain measured by random cross-validation is thus not a conservative estimate of the gain at new locations and times: it is an estimate of a different, interpolation-based, quantity.

\paragraph{Blocked cross-validation.} Blocking is necessary but not sufficient. With blocked folds, procedures that assume independent observations (i.i.d. bootstrap, $55\%$ at the moderate level) or ignore cross-sectional correlation (DM--HAC, $50\%$) are even worse than under random cross-validation, because the dependent test errors are no longer diluted across folds. The cluster procedures over-reject at every level, including without dependence ($11\%$ and $8\%$), since the fold effects induced by refitting XGBoost are correlated across blocks of the same fold. The Hering--Genton test, valid in Experiment~1, now rejects $17\%$ of true nulls under moderate and $23\%$ under strong dependence: it models the spatial correlation of the differentials but not the training-induced component, which is large in this design. DA-P is the best procedure at every level ($2.3\%$, $4.7\%$, $10.0\%$, $11.0\%$), ahead of the paired $t$-test ($5.3\%$, $7.7\%$, $14.3\%$, $14.3\%$) and DA-K ($3.3\%$, $7.0\%$, $15.0\%$, $14.3\%$), but it is not calibrated under moderate and strong dependence.

The residual over-rejection has a clear source. Whichever of the two covariate fields happens to be more variable over the whole domain in a given sample favours the corresponding model in every fold; with practical ranges of a quarter to a half of the domain (median range ratio $0.27$ and $0.36$), this sample-wide component acts as a fold effect that is common to all folds, i.e.\ as a large correlation $\rho$ between training effects. By Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}, it is then underestimated, and by the result of Bengio and Grandvalet~\cite{bengio2004} it cannot be estimated from a single cross-validation run. Consistently, assuming $\rho_0=0.5$ lowers the rejection rate to $8.7\%$ at both levels, and assuming $\rho_0=0$ raises it to $11.7\%$ and $15.3\%$; the three-way rule of Section~\ref{sec:rule}, evaluated in Table~\ref{tab:rule}, inherits the $8.7\%$.

\paragraph{Power.} Under the alternatives (right part of Table~\ref{tab:exp2}), DA-P and the blocked paired $t$-test have similar power at the moderate level ($63\%$ and $67\%$ for $\gamma=0.2$), lower than that of the invalid procedures. Without dependence, DA-P is less powerful than the paired $t$-test for large effects ($80\%$ against $100\%$ for $\gamma=0.3$), because a large fold-level variance $\hat\tau^2$, estimated with $K-1$ degrees of freedom, then dominates its variance and reduces its degrees of freedom.
