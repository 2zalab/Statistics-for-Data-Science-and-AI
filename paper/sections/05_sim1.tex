\section{Simulation study I: controlled loss-differential fields}\label{sec:sim1}

The first study isolates the inferential problem from the learning problem. Prediction errors are simulated directly, so that the true expected loss differential is known exactly and the dependence of the $d_i$ is controlled.

\subsection{Design}

\paragraph{Fields.} Observations lie on a $12\times12$ grid of sites observed at $24$ time points ($n=3456$). All fields are separable Gaussian fields with unit variance, exponential spatial correlation $\exp(-h/a)$ and AR(1) temporal dependence with coefficient $\phi$. Four dependence levels are considered: \emph{none} ($a=0$, $\phi=0$), \emph{weak} ($a=1$, $\phi=0.3$), \emph{moderate} ($a=2$, $\phi=0.6$) and \emph{strong} ($a=4$, $\phi=0.8$). The corresponding practical ranges are $3a=0,3,6,12$ grid units in space and $-3/\log\phi=0,2.5,5.9,13.4$ time steps; at the strong level the range is as large as the domain.

\paragraph{Losses.} With $\varepsilon$, $e_A$ and $e_B$ three independent fields, the response noise is $\varepsilon$, model $A$ has errors $e_A$ and model $B$ has errors $\sqrt{1+\delta}\,e_B$. Under squared loss,
\[
d_i=(\varepsilon_i-e_{A,i})^2-(\varepsilon_i-\sqrt{1+\delta}\,e_{B,i})^2+\eta_{k(i)},\qquad \E[d_i]=-\delta ,
\]
so that $\Delta_n=-\delta$ exactly. The fold effects $\eta_k=\sigma_\eta(\sqrt{\rho}\,g+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,h_k)$, with $g,h_1,\dots,h_K$ i.i.d. standard normal, $\sigma_\eta=0.05$ and $\rho=0.25$, mimic the training-induced variability of~\eqref{eq:model}, including its correlation across folds. We use $\delta=0$ (null) and $\delta\in\{0.1,0.2,0.4\}$ (alternatives).

\paragraph{Validation and procedures.} The domain is cut into $4\times4$-site tiles crossed with $6$-step time windows ($G=36$ blocks), which are allocated to $K=5$ folds with balanced sizes. We compare the paired $t$-test on fold means, the i.i.d. bootstrap, the block bootstrap and a random-intercept LMM (both with the $36$ blocks as clusters), the Diebold--Mariano test with a site-wise Newey--West variance (DM--HAC), and DA-HAC. Three ablations of DA-HAC are included: without the training term ($\hat\tau^2=0$), with a normal reference distribution, and with a fixed bandwidth equal to the block size instead of the variogram-calibrated one. Bootstraps use $999$ resamples. There are $1000$ replicates per dependence level under the null and $300$ under each alternative, so that the Monte Carlo standard error of a rejection rate of $5\%$ is $0.7$ percentage points.

\paragraph{Pilot.} The bandwidth multiplier $\kappa$ and the reference distribution were chosen in a pilot study with $300$ null replicates per level, run on random seeds disjoint from those of the main study. Rejection rates of DA-HAC varied by at most $2.7$ percentage points across $\kappa\in\{0.5,1,1.5,2\}$ at any level, with no systematic trend, and we retained $\kappa=1$. Capping the degrees of freedom by the kernel term of~\eqref{eq:df} reduced the rejection rate at the strong level from $17.7\%$ to $15.3\%$ without affecting the other levels by more than $1.4$ points, and was retained. The pilot results are included in the code repository.

\subsection{Results}

\paragraph{Type I error.} Table~\ref{tab:exp1} and Fig.~\ref{fig:exp1size} report rejection rates under the null. Procedures that ignore dependence fail quickly: the i.i.d. bootstrap rejects a true null in $19\%$ of replicates under weak dependence and in $80\%$ under strong dependence, and the time-only DM--HAC test, which ignores correlation between sites, behaves almost as badly ($17\%$ and $70\%$). The two cluster procedures, block bootstrap and LMM, are much better but still drift upwards as the range of dependence approaches the block size ($15\%$ and $14\%$ at the moderate level, $27\%$ and $25\%$ at the strong level), because they treat neighbouring blocks as independent. Even without any spatial or temporal dependence they reject $9\%$ and $7\%$ of true nulls, as they do not account for the correlation of fold effects. The paired $t$-test on blocked fold means is the best conventional procedure, in line with Proposition~\ref{prop:pairedt}, since blocking removes most of the covariance between fold means; it nevertheless reaches $11\%$ at the moderate and $18\%$ at the strong level.

DA-HAC has rejection rates of $4.8\%$, $7.2\%$, $9.9\%$ and $12.8\%$ at the four levels. It is the procedure closest to the nominal level without dependence and under moderate and strong dependence; under weak dependence the paired $t$-test ($6.7\%$) is marginally closer. Its ablation with a fixed bandwidth equal to the block size is slightly better at the weak and moderate levels ($5.6\%$ and $8.8\%$). That variant uses knowledge of a sensible block size, which the variogram calibration recovers from the data. DA-HAC is \emph{not} exactly calibrated under moderate and strong dependence. At the strong level the practical range ($12$ sites and $13$ time steps) equals the extent of the domain; the median estimated range ratio $b/L$ is $0.52$, the sample contains only a few effectively independent regions, and the estimated ranges are about half the true ones because the variogram cannot be estimated beyond the domain. In this regime no method we studied is reliable, and the range ratio is an informative warning (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Ablations.} Removing the training term $\zeta_K\hat\tau^2$ almost triples the rejection rate at the weak and moderate levels ($14\%$ and $24\%$ instead of $7\%$ and $10\%$): fold-centred kernel estimators miss the between-fold component of the variance, whose estimation is therefore essential. Replacing the Satterthwaite reference by a normal distribution adds up to $3$ percentage points, the largest increase being at the strong level.

\paragraph{Coverage, width and power.} Coverage of the $95\%$ intervals over all replicates (null and alternatives, Table~\ref{tab:exp1}) mirrors the type I error: $95.4\%$, $92.6\%$, $89.7\%$ and $85.6\%$ for DA-HAC, against $79.7\%$, $43.0\%$ and $19.3\%$ for the i.i.d. bootstrap from the weak level on. The DA-HAC intervals are wider than those of the cluster procedures (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp1size}b), which is the price of validity: the narrow intervals of the i.i.d. bootstrap and DM--HAC do not widen with dependence at all. Raw rejection rates under the alternatives (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp1power}) are not comparable across procedures whose sizes differ by an order of magnitude; we therefore also report size-adjusted power, using as critical value the $5\%$ quantile of each procedure's null $p$-values (Table~\ref{tab:exp1power}). DA-HAC has about the same size-adjusted power as the paired $t$-test. Without dependence it is less powerful than the cluster procedures ($61\%$ against $81\%$ at $\delta=0.2$), because the training term is estimated from only $K-1=4$ degrees of freedom; under moderate dependence the cluster procedures retain more size-adjusted power, but this advantage cannot be realised in practice, since their nominal tests over-reject and their null distribution is unknown.
